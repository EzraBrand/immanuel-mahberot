# Immanuel of Rome, *Maḥberot*: the Second Maḥberet (Tamar and Beriah)

Pilot translation, 13-Nov-2026.

**Source text:** Project Ben-Yehuda, <https://benyehuda.org/read/36886> (text of the Yarden edition, Mosad Bialik, 1957), section "המחברת השניה". The Hebrew is not copied here.

## Method of this pilot

- **Rhymed prose (the frame story):** translated as cadenced prose. Rhyme is kept only where English gives it without added words. The Hebrew rhymes all through.
- **The 90 alternating sayings:** one English sentence for each Hebrew rhymed pair. They are numbered for reference; the numbers are not in the Hebrew.
- **The four poems:** a literal line-for-line version. For the first sonnet there is also one verse attempt, with a list of what the rhyme added.
- **Allusions:** given in square brackets after the phrase. They are identified from memory, not from Yarden's notes, and they are not checked against a concordance. Treat each as a claim to verify.
- **Names:** *Tamar* is the beautiful woman. *Beriah* (בְּרִיעָה) is the ugly woman; the name is in 1 Chr 7:23, where it is explained as "in evil" (בְּרָעָה). *The Prince* (הַשָּׂר) is Immanuel's patron. *Time* (הַזְּמָן) is fate, as in all medieval Hebrew poetry.
- **⚠** marks a rendering that I am not sure of. The list is at the end.

## The Second Maḥberet

Said Immanuel the author: See now, I have taken it upon me to speak [Gen 18:27]. When God made me wander from my father's house [Gen 20:13], and Time led me to the house of the Prince, the Western Lamp [the lamp of the Temple menorah that did not go out; b. Shabbat 22b], whose name is known in Judah and great in Israel [Ps 76:2], to seat me with nobles, the nobles of his people [Ps 113:8], we took our fill of love's delights [Prov 7:18], and talked of sweet songs and of the forceful flights [Job 6:25] of rhetoric, the glory of all lands [Ezek 20:6]. All who saw us ran up and took their stands [Ps 59:5], for what had not been told them they saw, and what they had not heard they understood [Isa 52:15]. The poets of the age remembered us for good; they bore us on the shoulder, they carried us [Isa 46:7]; with their mouths they raised our stature to the sky [Job 20:6] and set our throne above the stars of God on high [Isa 14:13]. The nobles of the peoples gathered [Ps 47:10] and longed to carry off some of our sayings [Deut 33:3]; and when our spirit rested on them, they prophesied and did not cease [Num 11:25]. They raised our praise upon their lips, and said that they saw in us what they had seen in no one else.

One day we went up to the gate, to the elders [Deut 25:7], and there sat an assembly of princes and officers, and a company of the wise of heart and the discerning. When they saw us from afar [Gen 37:18], before we came near them, their mouth was filled with laughter [Ps 126:2], and they opened their mouths without measure [Isa 5:14]. They said: "Let them come, let them come with singing, the bearers of the sheaves of understanding [Ps 126:6]! How honoured is this session today, and how fair its array [Job 41:4], now that fortune has brought the chief of the poets this way!" When we heard their words, pride sprouted in our heart, for Time had set them before us to gaze upon [Ezek 28:17]. We bowed to them with courtesy and humility, and we said, each man to his brother: "Let us make us a name [Gen 11:4], with songs and figures that we shall make new ⚠, and with noble riddles that came to us as a heritage, and we inherited them; so that they may know that we have hallowed them [Ezek 20:12]."

While we sat at the head of the companies, a company of women came passing by, and at their head two ladies in royal apparel, blue and white, with a great crown of gold on their heads [Esth 8:15] and ornaments of gold upon their apparel [2 Sam 1:24]. The one filled the world with the radiance of her beauty; had she lived in the days of Noah, he would have made her the window of the ark [Gen 6:16]. She was like a pavement of sapphire, and like the very heaven for clearness [Exod 24:10]. She found favour in the eyes of all who saw her [Esth 2:15], and we stood and wondered at her [Gen 24:21]. Had Eldad and Medad been in her camp, they would have prophesied by her spirit [Num 11:26]. And the second: wrath, and fury, and trouble [Ps 78:49]; her form was darker than black [Lam 4:8]. In fury she marches through the earth [Hab 3:12], and "Unclean, unclean!" she cries [Lev 13:45]. When I saw her from afar I thought to bow the knee; when I came near her I said: "A time to rend!" [Eccl 3:7]. And the elders and all the people said: "Break the arm!" [Ps 10:15]. I have not seen her like for badness in all the land of Egypt [Gen 41:19]. The men looked at one another in wonder [Gen 43:33], and each one, though laughter was sweet in his mouth, held it back under his palate [Job 20:12-13], for the pain was very great [Job 2:13] in the eyes of the elders when they saw the accursed woman [2 Kgs 9:34] in perfumes and spices of every kind [2 Chr 16:14] and in vessels each different from the other [Esth 1:7]. The whole congregation said to stone her with stones [Num 14:10], for they said: "If this one lives, she will turn the world back to chaos. Has there ever been such a great thing as this, or has the like of it been heard?" [Deut 4:32]. And the elders and all the people said: "This one was born on account of the sin of Jeroboam. We will not return to our houses [Num 32:18] until we put forth our strength [Eccl 10:10], and speak about these ladies, and compose songs and figures upon them."

Then I said to the Prince, the honoured and the dread [Hab 1:7]: "By the two of them you shall be allied to me this day [1 Sam 18:21]. One of us shall praise the fair one, and the other shall shame the ugly one, and we shall go to the limit in praise and in blame, until all the fountains of song are broken up and the windows of rhetoric are opened [Gen 7:11], until all the daughters of song are brought low before us [Eccl 12:4]." The Prince said: "Since Time has graced you with a woman of grace [Prov 11:16], you shall be tested in the praise of the fair one. And I will see to the accursed woman [2 Kgs 9:34], and set my face to shame the ugly one, and offer strange fire before her [Lev 10:1]." Our words were good in the eyes of the hearers, and they bent their ears to hear our sayings. So I began to praise the fair one, and the Prince began to shame the ugly one and to give her her fill of reproach and reviling.

1. And I answered and said: Tamar lifts her eyes to heaven and brings out its host by number [Isa 40:26]; she looks to the earth and wakes those who sleep in the dust [Dan 12:2].
2. And the Prince said: Beriah kills the viper with her eyes; had Satan seen her, he would have fled from her face.
3. And I answered and said: Tamar: we have wearied of telling her splendour. Had she met the sons of the gods, they would have said to her: "Here we are, and we will go up!" [Num 14:40].
4. And the Prince said: Beriah is good, in the days of the first ripe grapes [Num 13:20], to hang upon the tree to drive the wolves away.
5. And I answered and said: Tamar: had the angels known her, they would have set her upon a pole to give life to the bitten [Num 21:8-9].
6. And the Prince said: Beriah: I wonder that the princes do not tie her to the lintel, to play with her in place of the ape.
7. And I answered and said: Tamar says to the rising sun: "Go up, bald head! Go up, bald head!" [2 Kgs 2:23].
8. And the Prince said: Beriah: had I remembered her at the death of my father, I would have set her before me [Ps 16:8] to stir up my pain.
9. And I answered and said: Tamar has breached a breach [Gen 38:29, of Perez, the son of the biblical Tamar] in the border of beauty; had the sons of the gods known her, they would not have left her on the earth [Gen 6:2].
10. And the Prince said: Beriah: Time would have killed her long ago with the arrow of lightning and the voice of thunder, had it not kept her to stir up mourning in the day of wrath.
11. And I answered and said: Tamar looks out from the window and says: "Sun, stand still at Gibeon, and moon, in the valley of Aijalon!" [Josh 10:12].
12. And the Prince said: Beriah: had I seen her at the beginning of the year, I would have been in terror, lest the evil cleave to me and I die [Gen 19:19].
13. And I answered and said: Tamar by her beauty repairs every breach [2 Kgs 12:6], and with her eyes puts Venus and Jupiter to shame.
14. And the Prince said: Beriah: had I remembered her when I lay with a doe upon a fresh green bed [Song 1:16], I would have had no pleasure [Gen 18:12] all that night.
15. And I answered and said: Tamar has taken from all splendours the glory of their beauty, and from the high virtues their topmost bough [Ezek 17:3].
16. And the Prince said: Beriah: had all the gazelles been like her, there would have been no need to command us about forbidden unions.
17. And I answered and said: Tamar: her beauty is majestic; the lights of heaven grow dark [Joel 2:10], and she does not grow dark.
18. And the Prince said: Beriah: were she set among the spheres, at her rising the mighty would be afraid [Job 41:17].
19. And I answered and said: Tamar excels in beauty, in rank and in manners; the moon wanes, and she does not wane.
20. And the Prince said: Beriah: I wonder that the Jews do not hold her before them when they read Lamentations, and that the demons have not agreed to make her their queen.
21. And I answered and said: Tamar: her pedestals were sunk upon precious stones [Song 5:15; Job 38:6]. I would choose to be a flea, and to die between her fingernails.
22. And the Prince said: Beriah was created only to grieve those who see her, and to bring down the helpless by her might [Ps 10:10].
23. And I answered and said: Tamar: would that I were dust and she would tread on me, or an ass, and she would draw me with cords of love [Hos 11:4].
24. And the Prince said: Beriah: I wonder if the Jews will look for their Messiah while she lives, and if the land will not bereave its nations in her days [Ezek 36:13-14].
25. And I answered and said: Tamar: there is no good in Time like the sight of her splendour, and no punishment has been found like parting from her.
26. And the Prince said: Beriah frightens those who see her and puts their hope to shame ⚠ [Zech 9:5], and even when she is silent she says: "Behold, I have come out as an adversary" [Num 22:32].
27. And I answered and said: Tamar: I went out at midnight once and twice, to see if among the stars of heaven we have an exchange for her eyes.
28. And the Prince said: Beriah is the reproach of all her nation, and I would wonder if they speak the Name in her presence.
29. And I answered and said: I marvelled when I saw the sun grow dark with no cause to compel it, until I saw Tamar standing where the paths meet [Prov 8:2].
30. And the Prince said: The shame of Beriah exceeds every shame, and reproach and reviling are not enough for her.
31. And I answered and said: Had we seen Tamar only one time in a month, we would have bowed down to the Lord in the splendour of holiness [Ps 29:2; and the saying on the blessing of the new moon, b. Sanhedrin 42a].
32. And the Prince said: Beriah stores up every blemish and every reproach, and her mouth is like the mouth of a pig and like passing dung [b. Berakhot 25a].
33. And I answered and said: I marvelled when I looked at the heavens and Venus was not there, until I saw Tamar, who carries her in her face.
34. And the Prince said: Had Beriah lived in the days of Joshua, she would have been brought to stand for the curse upon Mount Ebal [Deut 27:13].
35. And I answered and said: Had Tamar been set in the heavens, the nations would not have worshipped the sun and the moon.
36. And the Prince said: Were I high priest in Israel, I would send Beriah into the wilderness, to Azazel [Lev 16:10].
37. And I answered and said: Had I hoped to find my desires in Tamar after my death, I would have said: "My death is better than my life" [Jonah 4:3].
38. And the Prince said: Beriah: no angel formed her, and no separate intellect, for everyone who sees her is appalled and trembles.
39. And I answered and said: Tamar was brought up at the side of Beauty [Esth 2:20]; had the sun known her, he would not have joined himself to the moon.
40. And the Prince said: Beriah: the sons of the gods made her on the day of the great slaughter, when the towers fall [Isa 30:25].
41. And I answered and said: Tamar: had Moses lived in her time, she would have been Moses' portion [Lev 8:29].
42. And the Prince said: Beriah: her mother conceived her on a day of gloom, darkness, cloud and thick darkness [Deut 4:11].
43. And I answered and said: Tamar: had Aaron known her, he would have offered himself to her, and he would not have taken Elisheba the daughter of Amminadab [Exod 6:23].
44. And the Prince said: I saw Beriah, and she sent the sons of her quiver into my kidneys [Lam 3:13]. I will not tell her disgrace, lest I make her shame less than it is ⚠.
45. And I answered and said: Tamar is the perfection of beauty and the joy of every city [Lam 2:15]. Had she lived in the days of Solomon, he would not have made Bithiah queen [Pharaoh's daughter, 1 Kgs 3:1; the name is from 1 Chr 4:18].
46. And the Prince said: He who dies on one day with Beriah is happy, for the demons will be so busy with her that they will turn back from the chase after him.
47. And I answered and said: Tamar is the Eden of Time, and there is none besides her. Had Bezalel known her, he would have made the cherubim after her pattern.
48. And the Prince said: In Beriah there are blemishes that every tongue would weary to tell. As I live, if the dead saw her, they would flee from their graves.
49. And I answered and said: Tamar hides the sun whenever she goes out, as the sun in its radiance hides the light of the stars.
50. And the Prince said: I say: "My God! Kill Beriah and let me live, or let her live and kill me!"
51. And I answered and said: Tamar is the crown of her time, and from the lampstand of Bezalel she stole its base and its shaft [Exod 25:31; the words also mean "her thigh and her stalk"].
52. And the Prince said: Redeem me, God, from Beriah, whom you have stationed at my right hand [Ps 109:6]; and if not, blot me, I pray, out of your book that you have written! [Exod 32:32].
53. And I answered and said: Tamar is of my choice fruits and my finest nard [Song 4:13]. I would embrace her, even if the tablets of the covenant were upon my two hands [Deut 9:15].
54. And the Prince said: As I live, Beriah was not born of a man and a woman, but of mould and stench and rot [Joel 2:20].
55. And I answered and said: Tamar: her beauty, and not the beauty of Bathsheba the wife of Uriah; the clearness of her sayings, and not the words of Isaiah.
56. And the Prince said: Time says while Beriah lives: "She was born for my grief" [Prov 17:21]; and at her death it will say: "God has taken away my reproach" [Gen 30:23].
57. And I answered and said: Had Tamar been among the angels on high, their soul would have panted for her, and they would not have accepted the commandment "You shall not commit adultery."
58. And the Prince said: Beriah: I wonder if the world will bear her, and if the earth will bury her after her death.
59. And I answered and said: Tamar: had our God created her alone in the six days of creation ⚠, "this is the Lord's doing; it is marvellous in our eyes" [Ps 118:23].
60. And the Prince said: I wonder if laughter is to be found in the time of Beriah, and if there is peace for the near and for the far [Isa 57:19].
61. And I answered and said: If all things that exist came to be by the hand of the apprentice in one instant of time, the creation of Tamar took two instants, by the hand of the master ⚠.
62. And the Prince said: Beriah was created only to tear prey, and to be a name of reproach to the sons of her people.
63. And I answered and said: Tamar: lovers walk by her light and set their road toward her, as if they had been commanded about her at that hour from Sinai.
64. And the Prince said: Beriah with her face stirs up mourning, as the harp and the lute stir up joy.
65. And I answered and said: Tamar: the hope of lovers would perish if they lost her, and were the fear of God not upon their faces [Exod 20:17], they would worship her.
66. And the Prince said: Beriah says whenever she goes out: "Flee, my brothers, for no man shall see me and live!" [Exod 33:20].
67. And I answered and said: Tamar: if the angels on high did not hope to see her image, they would come down to the earth to dwell upon the ground ⚠.
68. And the Prince said: Beriah: if she were not marked out by her robes, I would not know her from the bat.
69. And I answered and said: Tamar: had Amnon seen her in a dream, he would not have made himself sick for Tamar the sister of Absalom [2 Sam 13:2].
70. And the Prince said: I wonder how Beriah takes her place among the ladies, and does not dwell with the weasel and the mouse and the lizard [Lev 11:29].
71. And I answered and said: Tamar: had there been in Egypt a woman of her beauty and her manners, the princes would not have praised Sarah to Pharaoh [Gen 12:15].
72. And the Prince said: He who hears laughter from the mouth of Beriah, let him mourn and weep, for he is appointed to death; and if it tarries, let him wait for it [Hab 2:3].
73. And I answered and said: Tamar: I would wonder if a man dies while she is still among the living, or if a person lives after her death.
74. And the Prince said: If Time has a nakedness, Beriah is its nakedness; and if there is rot in Time, she is its rot and its stench.
75. And I answered and said: Had Joseph seen Tamar, he would have said: "I will run after you" [Song 1:4], and he would not have left his garment in her hand, and fled, and gone outside [Gen 39:12].
76. And the Prince said: Beriah whets her tongue like a dog [Exod 11:7], "because of uncleanness that destroys, with a grievous destruction" [Mic 2:10].
77. And I answered and said: Tamar: before her radiance the Pleiades were ashamed, and the moon was confounded, and the sun was ashamed [Isa 24:23].
78. And the Prince said: Were Beriah to dwell in the holes of the dust and the rocks [Job 30:6], trembling would take hold of the bats.
79. And I answered and said: Tamar: had the Virgin who is in the heavens been made after her pattern, the sun would never journey from the Virgin to the Scales.
80. And the Prince said: Beriah: were they to strip her of her embroidered garments [Ezek 26:16], she would clothe the heavens in blackness and make sackcloth the covering of the earth [Isa 50:3].
81. And I answered and said: Tamar: had the Everlasting God made her in the heavens, they would quarrel over her, and there would be no peace in his high places [Job 25:2].
82. And the Prince said: Beriah would not have been married if her husband were not blind; and if it is not so, let us believe that he was struck with blindness [Gen 19:11].
83. And I answered and said: Tamar: happy is the soul that is alive while she is; and great is the punishment of the soul that stays alive after her.
84. And the Prince said: Beriah: her disgrace has burst the bounds of all disgrace, and it is no wonder if in her lifetime no vision breaks through [1 Sam 3:1].
85. And I answered and said: Tamar: when she came, we were in Eden, the garden of God [Ezek 28:13]; and after she parted from us, we were left astonished.
86. And the Prince said: I have heard that the Messiah hides for fear of Beriah, and his city is shut up tight because of her; none goes out and none comes in [Josh 6:1].
87. And I answered and said: Every generation has its wonder, and Tamar is the wonder of our time, which our fathers and our fathers' fathers did not see [Exod 10:6].
88. And the Prince said: How long shall we revile and curse? Let us flee, lest Beriah come back and lay us among the slain!
89. And I answered and said: It is hard for me to be silent about the splendour of the fair one, for she is the balm and the cure for the bite of Beriah.
90. And the Prince said: You have spoken well, for if Tamar were not the balm for our bite, the days of our weeping would never pass [Deut 34:8].

When the people heard our rhetoric, they praised our brilliance and said: "Who would have believed our report [Isa 53:1], that there would be a blessing this day round about our hill?" [Ezek 34:26]. They blessed us and said: "You have breached a breach in the border of rhetoric. Blessed are you of the Lord, who made heaven and earth! [Ps 115:15]. By the hearing of the ear we had heard of the beauty of your rhetoric [Job 42:5], and behold, the half of the greatness of your wisdom was not told us [2 Chr 9:6]. To you alone was rhetoric given [Job 15:19]; you have entered the forest of its garden-land and its farthest lodging [2 Kgs 19:23], and the rhetoric of every other speaker has become a breached city [Prov 25:28], and none but you has reached the half of it." Then the elders and the nobles said to us: "Will you not compose songs upon the ladies?" The Prince turned to me and said: "Compose a song in words of love and in sweet words, and set the fair one in the sphere of Aravot [the seventh heaven; a play on *arevot*, 'sweet']. And I will see to the accursed woman, and compose a song to shame the ugly one. Cursed is the ground for her sake!" [Gen 3:17]. When I heard the words of the Prince, I made ready the strings of my song upon the bowstring [Ps 11:2], and I set a crown on the head of the fair one.

And I took up my parable and said [Num 23:7]:

> See a doe, before whom the sun and the moon
> walk bowed down and darkened.
> So not for the light of the sun alone, but also
> for her light, I say: "Creator of the lights!" [the *Yotzer* blessing of the morning service]

When I had finished the song that they praised, the Prince said: "You have got off very lightly. Rise, compose a song that is equal to her praise [Prov 27:21], for my mind is to make an end of the ugly one." When I heard the words of the Prince, I made haste and did not delay [Ps 119:60], and I longed to compose a song to glorify the fair one.

And I took up my parable and said:

> All the does were created from the mire [b. Ḥullin 27b],
> at twilight [m. Avot 5:6], and under pressure;
> so, like a drop from a bucket and like fine dust [Isa 40:15],
> they were found, as thieves are, breaking in upon Time [Exod 22:1].
>
> But the making of my fawn, in truth, came forth
> with all the host on high and the mighty ones of the sky.
> She is the writing of God [Exod 32:16], and not upon an erasure [m. Avot 4:20];
> so the wonders of the doe are wonders indeed.
>
> I will not call the substance of the gazelle "matter,"
> for it is not like the matter of the does of the earth;
> its name is "the bearer of a lofty form."
>
> I will sing a new song, and decree a saying [Job 22:28],
> that she has breached a breach in all bounds,
> for her rank is hidden from man.

*The same sonnet, a verse attempt (Hebrew rhyme scheme abba abba cde cde):*

> All other does were fashioned out of mire,
> at twilight, in a squeeze, the hour late;
> so, like a bucket's drop, like dust, their weight:
> mere thieves whom Time caught tunnelling. But higher
>
> my fawn's own making, truly: with the choir
> of heaven's host and rams of sky, her date.
> God's writing she, and on no scraped slate;
> hence this doe's wonders make all wonder tire.
>
> I will not call the gazelle's substance "matter":
> it is not like the matter of earth's does;
> its name is "bearer of a lofty form."
>
> I'll sing new song, decree it, and not flatter:
> she breached all bounds, and where her station goes
> no man can know; it lies beyond our norm.

*Added for rhyme or meter, not in the Hebrew: "the hour late", "higher", "choir", "her date", "make all wonder tire", "and not flatter", "it lies beyond our norm".*

After I had said the song to its end, the Prince said: "You have put comfort in my heart. As I live, your song has come into my hand and given me pleasure [b. Avodah Zarah 16b]. I also will declare my opinion" [Job 32:10].

And he took up his parable and said:

> See a slave woman, burned with fire and cut down [Ps 80:17],
> in a wrap of fine linen, with a crown upon her head [Esth 8:15],
> who has come out this day to be my adversary [Num 22:32].
> Cursed is the ground for her sake!

He had not yet finished speaking the song [Gen 24:15] when he remembered the disgrace of the accursed woman, and how the Almighty had dealt bitterly with her [Ruth 1:20], and her skin that was scorched like an oven [Lam 5:10]. He rose from the midst of the congregation [Num 25:7], and in the heat of his spirit [Ezek 3:14] he grew bitter, and he lifted up his voice and said:

> Forming Intellect! On the day that you formed
> the daughter of Gershom ⚠, you put all that exists to shame,
> because in her body you made every disgrace complete;
> the dream of contempt came, and you interpreted it.
>
> Did you perhaps vow to gather every disgrace,
> or did you plan to make an owl and an ape?
> What is this dream that you have dreamed? [Gen 37:10]
> Has your spirit left you [1 Sam 16:14], or were you drunk?
>
> Tell me, anointed cherub [Ezek 28:14]! Were the stylus
> and the compass [Isa 44:13] stolen from you,
> so that you made the daughter of Gershom with a shovel [Isa 30:24]?
>
> Or did the stars on high work rebellion in her:
> did the Crab and the Scorpion stand at that hour
> at the Dragon's Tail, in the rising degree?

When the princes heard the songs and what they told, their mouth was filled with laughter, and the rest of the people who were at their feet [Exod 11:8] went down upon their knees; they shouted and fell on their faces [Lev 9:24]. After this the assemblies parted, and all the people were telling our praises, and speaking only of us, and tying upon their necks the rows of our jacinths and our topazes [Exod 28:17-19]. They praised God, from whom all wisdom and understanding come, and all the people went back, each to his house.

## Uncertain renderings (⚠)

| Place | Hebrew | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Frame, "figures that we shall make new" | בְּשִׁירִים וּבִמְלִיצוֹת נְחַדְּשֵׁם | The object of "renew" can be the songs or the name. |
| 26 | וְתוֹבִישׁ מֶבָּטָן | Read as "their hope" after Zech 9:5. There is possibly a second sense, "from the womb". |
| 44 | פֶּן אָקֵל מֵחֶרְפָּתָהּ | "lest I make her shame less" (words cannot equal it). An alternative is "lest I be made light by her shame". |
| 59 | אִלּוּ בְרָאָהּ ... אֱלֹהֵינוּ | The conditional has no clear main clause. "Her alone" is my addition to make sense. |
| 61 | בְּעִתַּת הַזְּמָן ... בִּשְׁתֵּי עִתּוֹת | Sense: Tamar needed twice the time, and the master's hand. The term עִתָּה as "instant" needs a check in Yarden. |
| 67 | לוּלֵי קִוּוּ ... לִרְאוֹת צַלְמָהּ | The logic of the conditional is not certain. |
| Second sonnet | בַּת־גֵּרְשֹׁם | Why Beriah is "the daughter of Gershom" is not known to me. Yarden's note is necessary. |

## Findings of the pilot

1. **The text is translatable at speed.** The Ben-Yehuda text is clean, and the vocalization removes most ambiguity.
2. **The allusion density is the main cost.** This maḥberet has about 130 identifiable allusions in about 1,900 words. Each must be verified. Yarden's printed notes (2 volumes, Mosad Bialik, 1957) would decrease this work very much.
3. **Rhyme:** a full rhymed-prose English version is possible, but each rhyme pair pushes toward added words. The verse attempt above added 7 phrases in 14 lines. A decision on this is necessary before more work.
4. **Content caution:** the dispraise of Beriah includes her dark colour ("darker than black", "slave woman burned with fire"). This is standard in the genre of *the beautiful and the ugly*, but a public edition needs an introduction that says so.
